## How it measures your weak spots

Every session you finish is replayed keystroke by keystroke in the app's core, written in Rust. Every character and every bigram, such as “qu” or “rr”, keeps an exponential moving average (EMA) of two things: errors, with a weight of 0.7, and slowness relative to your own pace, with a weight of 0.3. It does not compare you with anyone: a key is slow if it is slow for you.

## How it picks the next text

After each session, the engine takes the 2 or 3 worst units (you choose in Settings → Practice), with some randomness so it does not always repeat the same ones, and asks for a text with the right density of those units. Above the text you see what you are practicing; the app, whose interface is in Spanish, shows for example “Practicando: ñ · qu · z”.

The text comes from the local corpus, offline, or from the [optional AI](/en/features/ai) if you turn it on. While no session has been saved yet, the app starts with a diagnostic text.

## Keyboard heatmap

With the heatmap on, the on-screen keyboard tints each key by its score and marks it with a level from 1 to 5, so it does not depend on color alone. The legend names the levels: “Errores por tecla: 1 muy pocos, 2 pocos, 3 algunos, 4 bastantes, 5 muchos” (errors per key: 1 very few, 2 few, 3 some, 4 quite a few, 5 many).

## What you see at the end

The results screen shows net and gross WPM, accuracy, errors, missed characters and your updated weak spots. From there, “Siguiente texto” (next text, <kbd>Enter</kbd>) generates a new text for those weak spots and “Repetir” (repeat) goes back to the same one. Your progress stays in the [progress history](/en/features/progress).
