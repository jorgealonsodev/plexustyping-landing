## One beat per keystroke

Each click is one expected keystroke: at 60 BPM, one key per second. You choose the tempo between 30 and 300 BPM, the sound (“Clic”, “Madera” or “Bip”: click, wood or beep), the volume and an accent every N beats, up to 16. Changes apply at once. Next to the live statistics, a dot lights up on every beat, in another color on accented ones.

The sound comes from the app's core and only plays while a text is ready or in progress: it stops on the results screen and when you hide the window. <kbd>F4</kbd> turns it on and off from anywhere.

## Quick panel

Clicking the pulse dot opens a panel with what you adjust most while practicing: lower or raise the tempo, type it, the sound, the volume, the accent, the adaptive tempo and “Apagar F4” (turn off, F4).

## Rhythm offset

Every character you type records how many milliseconds it landed before or after the nearest click. The results show “Ritmo: desvío medio ±X ms” (rhythm: mean offset ±X ms) and what share of the keys were early or late.

## Adaptive tempo

With “Tempo adaptativo” (adaptive tempo) on (it comes off), every text typed in full at a single tempo adjusts the next one's:

- It goes up one step if accuracy reaches 97 % and the mean offset is no more than 15 % of the interval between beats.
- It goes down one step if accuracy is below 90 % or the offset exceeds 20 %.
- Otherwise it stays.

A step is 5 % of the tempo, with a minimum of 2 BPM. The results say it plainly: “Tempo: 60 → 63 BPM” or “Tempo: se mantiene en 60 BPM” (stays at 60 BPM). Each session's BPM stays in the [history](/en/features/progress).
