## Everything from the keyboard

The app is fully operable with the keyboard: <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves through the controls and <kbd>F6</kbd> goes to the header. Shortcuts cover the rest: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>,</kbd> opens the settings, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> searches for a setting, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>H</kbd> opens the history, <kbd>F2</kbd> the on-screen keyboard, <kbd>F4</kbd> the metronome and <kbd>F5</kbd> loads a new text.

## Screen reader

With “Screen reader announcements” on, in Settings → Accessibility, a polite `aria-live` region announces the next character after every keystroke, every wrong key, the start of every text and the results line. The announcements follow the interface language; in English: “next: a”, with names such as “space”, “line break” or “comma”, and “error: expected e, typed r”. Off, it announces nothing.

## Color-blind mode

“Colour-blind mode” changes the keyboard flashes to the blue `#0072B2` and the orange `#E69F00` of the Okabe–Ito palette, the same in every theme, and underlines the wrong key's label in bold. The heatmap already marks each key with its level, from 1 to 5, so it does not depend on color either.

## Contrast and type

All themes meet WCAG AA. “High contrast dark” and “High contrast light” reach 7:1 for text and 4.5:1 for the interface, and never dim the pending text. You can change the font, among them Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, Lexend Deca and OpenDyslexic, the size (80–200 %), the letter spacing and the line height. [Personalization](/en/features/personalization) explains it.

## Reduced motion

If your system asks for reduced motion, no transition or animation runs in the app. A pressed key is underlined in the flash color instead of flashing, the cursor stops blinking and the metronome dot still marks every beat, without a transition.
